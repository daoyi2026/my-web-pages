# Product and Visual Acceptance

The user's drawing defines the body silhouette. Never append undrawn limbs, ears, or belly meshes to create variety. Preserve drawn projections as part of the original outline. Colors are automatic; do not reintroduce a manual palette without a user request.

For plush-rendering changes, inspect the actual toy in a close and rotated view. Compare all active materials using the same outline, color, lighting and face. Differences must be visibly structural, not just labels, color changes or tiny length adjustments. Keep eyes and mouth legible under long pile. Prioritize a warm, cute expression over technical material complexity. Report implementation and verification separately from the user's visual acceptance.

Random small buttons, bows hats and barrettes are explicitly requested. Make them clearly visible, float hats above the complete fur silhouette and place bows in front with depth clearance, and keep the flat embroidered face clear. The primary page contains the drawing, companion, one random supportive sentence and card export; avoid returning decorative labels or technical controls.

Fabric styles mean whole-body cloth construction, not surface patches. Gingham must cover the complete front and back. Ribbon bows need broad folded fabric loops, tails and visible woven texture, not oval beads.

Never remove pile around accessories to avoid intersections. Separate accessory geometry from the full shell/pile envelope. Keep random surrounding props separate from the body and fit them into the exported frame.
